# Lock-in, data control and sovereignty: what the official record says

Compiled October 2026 from public sources. Every quotation below is verbatim from the source linked beside it. Quotations taken from video use YouTube's auto-captions of the broadcaster's own upload, so they keep the speaker's repetitions. Where a claim comes from a campaigner, an MP or the company, it is attributed to them and not stated as fact. Contract values are only those already in `data/contracts.csv`.

## 1. Buyers' own statements that switching supplier is hard

Several UK public bodies have stated in procurement notices that moving away from Palantir would be costly or technically difficult. These are the buyers' own justifications for awarding contracts without competition.

### National Energy System Operator (NESO), August 2026

NESO direct-awarded Palantir an "Interim Capability" contract (£21,240,324 inc. VAT as published; 8 Sep 2026 to 31 Jul 2027) under section 41 of the Procurement Act 2023 and Schedule 5 paragraph 6 ("absence of competition for technical reasons"). Its justification, in the award notice ([Find a Tender ocds-h6vhtk-06ed0c](https://www.find-tender.service.gov.uk/procurement/ocds-h6vhtk-06ed0c); raw record in `sources/raw/find-a-tender/ocds-h6vhtk-06ed0c.json`):

> "NESO currently runs two critical business processes - Connections and Skip Rates using the Palantir Foundry platform which is underpinned by proprietary architecture licensed exclusively by Palantir, Palantir possesses the specialist knowledge, technical expertise and the system knowledge required to maintain and support the solution effectively. Transitioning to an alternative supplier is not possible without significant cost, resource commitment and delivery risk to meet regulatory obligations. It would require the replacement of or significant redevelopment of the current data analytics environment, including migration of data, security accreditation of the replacement solution to the required standards, and training of NESO personnel."

NESO's stated position is that this is a stopgap: the notice says the contract "has been put in place to enable continuity of the existing critical platform services whilst a competitive procurement is undertaken", and that a Pre-Market Engagement notice "for the strategic modelling capability has been published".

### Ministry of Defence, December 2025

The MoD direct-awarded a follow-on Enterprise Agreement (£240,600,000 inc. VAT as published; 1 Apr 2026 to 31 Mar 2029) under section 41, Schedule 5 paragraphs 6 and 7 ([Find a Tender ocds-h6vhtk-05f7e9](https://www.find-tender.service.gov.uk/procurement/ocds-h6vhtk-05f7e9)). The transparency notice says:

> "This is because MoD’s data analytics capabilities use Palantir data analytics architecture that only Palantir is able licence, and which only Palantir has the design familiarity and technical expertise to fully support. Changing supplier for this requirement would involve rebuild of the underlying data analytics architecture needing support; reaccreditation of the new solutions at the required security levels; and retraining of MoD personnel; at significant cost (including to the current of level capability and interoperability with NATO and allied partners), diversion of resource, and disruption to in-train military operations and planning."

In a written answer on 17 February 2026 (PQ 111951, asked by Martin Wrigley MP), defence minister Luke Pollard confirmed the direct award under those two paragraphs and said "The decision to award this contract was the Secretary of State for Defence’s" ([Parliament written questions API](https://questions-statements-api.parliament.uk/api/writtenquestions/questions/1879654?expandMember=true)). The Commons Science, Innovation and Technology Committee has asked the government to "set out the reasons for awarding a £240 million Ministry of Defence contract to Palantir without a competitive tender process" (recommendation 98, [report HC 61, 3 June 2026](https://committees.parliament.uk/publications/53352/documents/298462/default/)).

### NHS England, 2022 and 2023: extension, then a "Transition & Exit" contract, then the FDP award

- **August 2022.** NHS England extended the Covid-era Foundry "Data Platform Service" to 11 June 2023 (£11.5m as published). Its notice said: "A change of contractor at this stage would cause significant inconvenience and have substantial duplication of cost implications as in order to compliantly replace the contractor now would require two concurrent procurement processes to be run for the same service. It would therefore be both impractical and uneconomic." ([Contracts Finder 2cf9dde2](https://www.contractsfinder.service.gov.uk/Notice/2cf9dde2-3991-4b41-8971-a9ee7ec432e2))
- **June 2023.** NHS England awarded Palantir a 12-month "Palantir Foundry Transition & Exit Contract" (£24,925,000 as published, 12 Jun 2023 to 11 Jun 2024) "for the transition from the Palantir Foundry platform to the new Federated Data Platform & Associate Services (FDP-AS) supplier". The notice describes "the smooth transition and exit service of critical products that were developed to respond to the Covid-19 pandemic (for Covid-19 and elective recovery purposes), to alternative provisions including the transition of products to the new FDP-AS supplier", and states that the FDP-AS "is a separate contract and procurement process" ([Contracts Finder d1df0a11](https://www.contractsfinder.service.gov.uk/Notice/d1df0a11-60ad-4fa5-b375-986b972bc006)).
- **November 2023.** The FDP-AS contract was signed with a Palantir-led consortium on 22 November 2023 (£182,242,760 as published, "not a committed amount"; forecast up to £330m) ([Find a Tender ocds-h6vhtk-0328ce](https://www.find-tender.service.gov.uk/procurement/ocds-h6vhtk-0328ce)). So the incumbent was paid to prepare an exit to whichever supplier won, and the incumbent's consortium won. The Register reported that "some use cases will be ported from the old system to the FDP" ([The Register, 25 Mar 2024](https://www.theregister.com/2024/03/25/nhs_palantir_contract_republished/)).

NHS England's position is that the FDP procurement was "rigorous, competitive" and run "following an open tender process where any eligible supplier could respond" ([NHS England FDP contract explainer](https://www.england.nhs.uk/digitaltechnology/nhs-federated-data-platform/security-privacy/contract-explainer/)). Louis Mosley has said NHS England "ran an 18-monthlong open competitive tender" with "30 independent assessors" ([Channel 4 News, 10 Jun 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uhaSgbWmOEs)).

## 2. Who controls the data

### NHS England's stated position (FDP)

From the [FDP contract explainer](https://www.england.nhs.uk/digitaltechnology/nhs-federated-data-platform/security-privacy/contract-explainer/):

- Palantir "is a “processor” under data protection law and the agreement, which means it only processes NHS data as directed by an NHS user."
- "all data is stored, processed and accessed within the UK – there is no overseas processing of any type of data, all data is held in UK data centres and doesn’t leave the UK, and it is a contractual requirement that personal data stored in the NHS FDP and NHS-PET (Privacy Enhancing Technology) cannot be accessed by its provider’s personnel or contractors based outside the United Kingdom."
- Palantir cannot "commercialise or market NHS data, even on an anonymised basis" or use it "to develop or derive new Supplier products (for example by training an AI model on NHS data)".
- "Palantir has no intellectual property rights (IPRs) in NHS data as part of this contract." NHS England owns commissioned IPRs, including the "Canonical Data Model", but "The platform itself is based on an existing Supplier data analytics product, Foundry, and so Palantir retains IPRs in that product."
- "Clear step in rights are included within the contract to ensure in the event of an extreme event the NHS can take control of the service and limit access to the data."

The FDP award notice says "The data platform will be owned and controlled by the NHS" ([Find a Tender ocds-h6vhtk-0328ce](https://www.find-tender.service.gov.uk/procurement/ocds-h6vhtk-0328ce)).

### Palantir's stated position

Louis Mosley, giving evidence to the Commons Science, Innovation and Technology Committee on 8 July 2025 ([transcript](https://committees.parliament.uk/oralevidence/16290/html/)):

- "the data controllers, the organisations that have legal responsibility, which in the NHS are typically trusts, maintain control over their data" (Q109). The chair replied: "You have not quite answered the question", which had been whether data could be "processed outside the United Kingdom or can be accessed or seized in any legal sense by any foreign Government".
- "In the NHS’s case, all the data is stored in the public cloud. It is stored in the London region of AWS, which is Amazon Web Services." (Q110)
- "the NHS’s instance of the software is not accessible to anyone outside the UK" (Q114), although "The software is developed and maintained with teams around the world, many of which are in the UK, but they are also in the US and in Singapore".
- "the logs associated with users in the NHS instances are not exported to the US in the way that is often typical" (Q115).
- On the MoD: "The data is theirs" (Q135), and "The deployment of software at secret, or above secret, is done on air-gapped networks. It is not on the public cloud, for example." (Q136)

He has since compared Palantir's position to Microsoft's: "we have no more access to it than say Microsoft has access to the contents of a word document or an Excel spreadsheet" ([Channel 4 News, 10 Jun 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uhaSgbWmOEs)).

### Where the record is contested

- In May 2026 the Financial Times reported, and the Guardian followed up, that NHS England had let Palantir and other contractor staff access patient data before pseudonymisation; an internal briefing referred to "unlimited access to non-NHSE staff" to part of the FDP ([Guardian, 11 May 2026](https://www.theguardian.com/society/2026/may/11/palantir-access-nhs-england-patient-data)). Mosley described this as "a specific technical permission inside one staging environment" held by "fewer than five Palantir employees who are UK-based, vetted and approved by NHS engineering leadership"; the Commons committee noted this was "despite him saying in April" that Palantir had no access to NHS data, and asked the government to confirm "the exact nature of Palantir’s access to identifiable and non-identifiable patient data, on what statutory basis this was authorised, when, and by whom; and whether the Information Commissioner was consulted" (recommendation 96, [HC 61](https://committees.parliament.uk/publications/53352/documents/298462/default/)).
- The Guardian reports the National Data Guardian, Dr Nicola Byrne, considered the NHS had broken its promise that identifiable patient information would be "limited to NHS staff with a legitimate need" ([Guardian editorial, 4 Jun 2026](https://www.theguardian.com/commentisfree/2026/jun/04/the-guardian-view-on-nhs-records-patients-are-not-raw-material-for-big-tech)).
- Palantir staff on the FDP were reported to have NHS.net email, SharePoint and Teams access ([Guardian, 8 Apr 2026](https://www.theguardian.com/society/2026/apr/08/alarm-health-service-palantir-staff-nhs-email-accounts)).
- On secondary uses, Mosley said in July 2026: "If the NHS were to choose to try and monetize that with drug companies for research purposes, that's a decision for the government, for the NHS, not for us. But it's absolutely something we could enable." ([The Spectator, 15 Jul 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1AZNf7eIkx0)). Alex Karp told the BBC in 2023 that a future sale of joined-up data would be "by the UK government, not by me" ([Digital Health, 30 Oct 2023](https://www.digitalhealth.net/2023/10/palantir-ceo-acknowledges-fdp-could-aid-nhs-data-being-sold-in-future/)).

### Ministry of Defence

The September 2025 MoD-Palantir strategic partnership release says Palantir will support the Defence Digital and Data Strategy and that "It will ensure data remains sovereign, freely available across MOD to be exploited wherever it is needed" ([GOV.UK, 18 Sep 2025](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/new-strategic-partnership-to-unlock-billions-and-boost-military-ai-and-innovation)). Mosley told Times Radio in June 2026: "We we're ultimately a US company. We're headquartered in America", and framed the question as whether "the British Ministry of Defense be absolutely certain that it has full operational control over its use of our software and that's where we have been through an exhaustive process to ensure that that's the case" ([Times Radio, 30 Jun 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IIr-2YOXZP8)).

## 3. US law and foreign-control exposure, including the CLOUD Act

- **Palantir.** Asked on Times Radio whether, under the US CLOUD Act, Palantir would have to hand over NHS data if the US government demanded it, Mosley said: "our understanding is no, we're not. Um but that would need to be tested in courts, of course, but no, we don't believe we are." He added: "We're not a cloud provider is the key point." ([Times Radio, 26 Aug 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=R1FLechZmFg)). As noted above, he told MPs the NHS data itself is hosted in the London region of AWS.
- **Palantir, on a US government instruction.** Asked on Sky News whether he would comply if a US president told Palantir to stop serving foreign governments, Mosley said "No, that that that couldn't be done. That would be a breach of contracts, it would be a breach of the law", and that the NHS and MoD "control and own their versions of Palantir software" ([Sky News, 22 Jun 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Sntfx0Q_7wQ)). In the same interview he called the US government's withdrawal of an Anthropic model "a canary in the coal mine" for dependence on US technology.
- **MPs.** Martin Wrigley MP warned that a US law "that can oblige tech companies to disclose information to American authorities may apply" to Palantir's FCA work, saying: "My concern is the FCA is doing very significant investigations into sensitive data using a foreign-controlled company that could be advised to pass data across to the US government." ([Guardian, 1 Jun 2026](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/jun/01/fcas-palantir-deal-could-expose-uk-financial-data-to-trumps-us-critics-fear)). Committee chair Dame Chi Onwurah MP told the Today programme in October 2026: "The key issue is we can’t be so dependent on one company. It’s a US company, subject to US legislation regarding access to our data." ([Guardian, 9 Oct 2026](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/oct/09/palantir-tom-watson-mob-rule-government-contracts)). The FCA later said it had not renewed the short-term contract ([Newsquawk, 6 Oct 2026](https://www.newsquawk.com/headlines/uk-regulator-fca-says-it-has-not-renewed-short-term-contract-with-palantir-pltr-on-technology-and-financial-crime)).
- **NHS England.** Dr Vin Diwakar told MPs: "As a general thing, you cannot export data to the US." ([8 Jul 2025 transcript](https://committees.parliament.uk/oralevidence/16290/html/), Q178). NHS England's explainer says the access restrictions "collectively ensure that NHS data remains under UK jurisdiction". Neither source addresses the CLOUD Act by name.
- **Committee.** The Commons Science, Innovation and Technology Committee concluded that "The UK’s reliance on a small number of US-based providers for digital infrastructure and public service delivery is a strategic and economic vulnerability" and that ambitions "could be derailed at any time by a decision taken outside our shores based on the narrow interests of a foreign commercial or state actor" (conclusion 121, [HC 61](https://committees.parliament.uk/publications/53352/documents/298462/default/)).

## 4. Exit terms and the 2027 decision

### What the FDP contract allows

- "only a three year term is committed, which can be extended by two years and then two further one year periods – giving the contract a potential end date of March 2031" ([NHS England explainer](https://www.england.nhs.uk/digitaltechnology/nhs-federated-data-platform/security-privacy/contract-explainer/)). The Commons committee gives the end of the initial commitment as 15 February 2027.
- NHS England lists its lock-in safeguards as the incremental term; the platform not being "integrated into NHS core systems"; "clear terms for migrating data and services to another supplier or back to the NHS if the contract ends"; in-house skills; and a "Solution Exchange" for other suppliers. It also says "Vendor lock-in is avoided through contractual limitations, a seven-year contract term, robust exit planning, standards and technical guidelines to utilise agnostic services that can be migrated."
- "Any decision to extend the contract will be taken by Ministers in line with governance and regulatory processes." Health minister Dr Zubir Ahmed told a Westminster Hall debate on 16 April 2026: "The FDP is, and will continue to be, an important component of delivering patient care in the NHS in England. Of course, who contracts with the FDP will be open to question as we go forward and think about future contractual arrangements." (quoted on the same NHS England page).

### What NHS England and the government have said about lock-in

- Dr Diwakar (NHS England) to MPs: "the contract with the consortium led by Palantir is a seven-year contract. At the end of that period, we will have to make a decision about what we procure next" and "There is always a risk of vendor lock-in in any procurement process ... We would want to avoid vendor lock-in." Committee member Martin Wrigley MP replied: "This is the point about avoiding lock-in, which I think we are currently walking towards with a blindfold on." ([8 Jul 2025](https://committees.parliament.uk/oralevidence/16290/html/), Q180-182)
- Amy Freeman, chief digital information officer at University Hospitals of North Midlands, agreed with Danny Beales MP that staff cuts raise the risk of being locked into contracts, "particularly if we want to do things at pace, because the way to get people in at pace is to use contracting and consultancy services" (Q155).
- The Financial Times reported in March 2026 that ministers had taken soundings on the break clause and that "Government officials have argued that it is feasible to transfer the running of the FDP, which Palantir is building, to another provider" ([Guardian, 31 Mar 2026](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/mar/31/palantir-uk-boss-louis-mosley-ministers-nhs-contract)).

### What Palantir has said

- To MPs, asked the risk of NHS lock-in: "Technically, I think it is extremely low." Pressed on what a customer keeps: "They own the data and the logic—the content of it", but "they would need to have some substitute for the connections". On dependency: "there is no technical lock-in, so the dependency is not technical. However, if our services are very useful and they enable the Government to deliver very important outcomes, arguably that creates a form of dependency." He cited a Cabinet Office border-flow programme which, when the contract ended, was able to "export the data and logic that they created in it to another system" ([8 Jul 2025](https://committees.parliament.uk/oralevidence/16290/html/), Q119-122, Q138).
- On BBC Radio 4's Today programme (June 2026): "So this notion that we are part of a vendor lock-in is false" ([Guardian, 8 Jun 2026](https://www.theguardian.com/politics/2026/jun/08/silicon-valley-meta-maga-politics-nick-clegg)).
- On Times Radio (Aug 2026): "Dependency and lock-in are different things." He also asked critics to name an alternative: "Is okay, cancel Palantir, but what are you going to use instead?" ([Times Radio, 26 Aug 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=R1FLechZmFg)).

### What critics and committees have said

- Medconfidential told the Commons committee Palantir would have "followed their commercial self-interests in attempting to burrow so deep into the NHS that they cannot be migrated away from and can name their fee" (quoted in [HC 61](https://committees.parliament.uk/publications/53352/documents/298462/default/), para 87).
- The committee recommended that "The government should commit to exercising the February 2027 break clause in the Federated Data Platform contract and either develop an in-house replacement or seek an alternative developed by UK-owned and UK-based providers", and "publish a fully costed exit plan for the Federated Data Platform by the end of 2026" (recommendation 95). It also concluded: "We believe that vendor lock-in should not be viewed as inevitable" (conclusion 106).
- The Commons Health and Social Care Committee separately urged the government to end the contract, citing "serious mistrust" ([Guardian, 9 Jul 2026](https://www.theguardian.com/politics/2026/jul/09/mps-urge-labour-to-ditch-330m-palantir-software-contract-with-nhs)).
- In the Metropolitan Police case, the mayor's office warned the Met "risked getting locked into Palantir’s tech" (as reported by [Novara Media, 21 May 2026](https://novaramedia.com/2026/05/21/sadiq-khan-blocks-50m-palantir-ai-deal-with-met-police/)); MOPAC's formal reasons were procurement process and value for money, and its October 2026 settlement statement said the decision "was not based on Palantir’s values or ethics or any wider reporting about the company" ([JC, 14 Oct 2026](https://www.thejc.com/news/uk/sadiq-khan-backs-down-from-comments-about-palantirs-values-and-settles-legal-battle-with-us-tech-firm-va1qrjrk)).

## 5. Open questions the record does not yet answer

- Whether the FDP break clause will be exercised before 15 February 2027, and whether a costed exit plan exists (the committee asked for one by end-2026).
- The full FDP contract terms on exit and data protection: the contract was first published heavily redacted in December 2023 and republished with fewer redactions in March 2024 after a Good Law Project pre-action letter ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2024/03/25/nhs_palantir_contract_republished/)). The Information Commissioner is investigating a DHSC refusal to release official reports about the FDP ([Guardian, 5 Feb 2026](https://www.theguardian.com/politics/2026/feb/05/calls-to-halt-uk-palantir-contracts-grow-amid-lack-of-transparency-over-deals)).
- Whether the CLOUD Act or other US law could reach data processed through Palantir software hosted on a US cloud provider in the UK. Palantir says it believes not, while accepting it "would need to be tested in courts"; no UK buyer has published a legal analysis.
- What NESO's and the MoD's competitive or follow-on procurements will look like, given both buyers' statements that only Palantir can currently support their systems.
