c) Deferred tax asset

A net deferred tax asset is recognised only if it can be regarded as more likely than not that there will be suitable taxable
profits from which the future reversal of the underlying timing differences can be deducted. The Company is currently not
recognising a deferred tax asset as it is more likely than not that a future benefit on such deferred tax assets will not be
realised.

d) Factors which may affect future tax charges

The main rate of corporation tax is 25% for companies with profits over £250,000 for the financial year beginning 1 April
2025 (previously 25% in the financial year beginning 1 April 2024). A 19% small profits rate applies to those with
augmented profits of £50,000 or less. Companies with profits between £50,000 and £250,000 pay the main rate, but this is
reduced by a marginal rate.

e) Pillar Two rules

The Group is subject to Pillar Two income taxes arising from the OECD’s global anti-base erosion (GIoBE) rules. The
UK enacted legislation through the Finance (No. 2) Act 2023 to implement these rules, including a Multinational Top-
up Tax (MTT) and a Domestic Top-up Tax (DTT) for accounting periods beginning on or after 31 December 2023,
together with an Undertaxed Profits Rule effective for accounting periods beginning on or after 31 December 2024.
These rules establish a minimum effective tax rate of 15% for large multinational enterprise groups and large-scale
domestic groups with consolidated annual revenues exceeding €750 million in at least two of the preceding four
accounting periods.
