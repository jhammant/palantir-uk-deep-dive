# Palantir Technologies UK, Ltd.: accounts and tax, 2010 to 2025

## What this covers

- Source: the annual accounts of Palantir Technologies UK, Ltd. (Companies House number 07042994) for every year it has filed, from the first period ending 31 December 2010 to the year ending 31 December 2025. The figures are in `data/tax.csv`. The text of every page used is saved as `sources/companies-house/accounts-<year>-page-<nnn>.ocr.txt`, next to the PDFs.
- Page numbers below are page numbers in the downloaded PDF, which differ from the numbers printed on the pages.
- The 2016 to 2019 accounts are group accounts: the company together with its subsidiaries (Palantir Technologies UK - Eagle, Ltd., and from 2018 a Qatar subsidiary). Those rows are group figures. All other years are the company alone.
- The 2011 filing is abbreviated small-company accounts with no profit and loss account; the 2011 figures come from the comparatives in the 2012 accounts.
- In the CSV, tax amounts are positive for a charge and negative for a credit; a loss is a negative profit.
- "Effective rate" here means the total tax charge divided by profit before tax. It is calculated from the accounts, not stated in them.

## The trend

- **2010 to 2015: a service provider to the US parent.** The accounts describe turnover as commission income from the parent company for sales and marketing services, recognised on a cost plus 7% basis (2010 accounts p9; 2012 p10; 2013 p10; 2014 p14). The 2014 and 2015 strategic reports say all turnover arises from a cost-plus arrangement with the parent (2014 p4; 2015 p5). Between 96% and 100% of turnover was attributed to markets outside the UK, mainly the USA (2010 p9; 2012 p11; 2013 p11; 2014 p15; 2015 p20). Profit before tax was a margin of about 4% to 7% of turnover. The tax charge was above the standard rate in 2013, 2014 and 2015, and below it in 2012 (18.8% against 24.5%); the 2010 and 2011 accounts state no standard rate.
- **2016 to 2021: still mostly parent services, with UK sales starting.** Between 88% and 95% of turnover in 2016 to 2020, and 72% in 2021, was attributed to markets outside the UK "for rendering of services to the Ultimate Parent" (2016 p20; 2017 p20; 2018 p22; 2019 p28; 2020 p26; 2021 p29), at a service fee of costs plus 7%, or 5% in 2020 and 2021 (2020 p4; 2021 p4). UK external sales grew from £4.2m in 2016 to £51.8m in 2021.
- **2022 to 2025: UK customer sales grow and parent services shrink.** Services to the parent fell from 55% of turnover in 2022 to 41% in 2023, 29% in 2024 and 3% in 2025 (2022 p28; 2023 p29; 2024 p30; 2025 p28). UK turnover rose from £87.0m to £236.4m. The service fee stayed at costs plus 7% (2025 p4). Profit before tax was £19.1m to £25.3m a year, and the tax charge was 4.0% to 8.2% of it, against standard rates of 19% to 25%.
- Average employees rose from 57 in 2013 to a peak of 842 in 2023, and were 771 in 2025.

## Why the tax charge is below the standard rate (2022 to 2025)

- **What the tax reconciliation shows.** In each year the two largest lines are "share option timing differences", which reduce the charge, and "deferred tax not recognised", which increases it. They largely cancel out, and the difference between them is about the gap between tax at the standard rate and the actual charge:
  - 2025: share option timing differences £145,814,123; deferred tax not recognised £140,931,917; tax at the standard rate £6,283,414; actual charge £1,900,613 (2025 p31).
  - 2024: £92,157,651 and £87,743,106; tax at the standard rate £6,328,551; actual charge £2,079,242 (2025 p31; 2024 p32).
  - 2023: £22,139,004 and £18,090,317; tax at the standard rate £4,496,606; actual charge £901,829 (2023 p31).
  - 2022: £3,846,412 and £330,835; tax at the standard rate £3,790,109; actual charge £803,017 (2022 p30).
  - Net, these two lines reduced the charge by £4.88m in 2025, £4.41m in 2024, £4.05m in 2023 and £3.52m in 2022 (calculated from the lines above).
- **The accounts' explanation.** The notes say: "A judgment has been made to exclude from the estimate of future taxable profits the possible tax losses arising from share-based payment expenses incurred on exercise of the awards in excess of the related grant date fair value" (2025 p24; the same wording is in 2020 p21, 2021 p24, 2022 p24, 2023 p25 and 2024 p25). They also say no deferred tax asset is recognised "as it is more likely than not that a future benefit on such deferred tax assets will not be realised" (2025 p31, note 5c).
- **In plain terms**, as the accounts present it: tax deductions for employee share awards, measured when the awards are exercised, were larger than the share-based payment expense in the accounts. They are shown as "share option timing differences". The accounts do not recognise a deferred tax asset for the possible tax losses this creates (2025 p24, p31), and in the reconciliation the "deferred tax not recognised" line largely offsets the share option line. The share-based payment expense in the accounts was £48.8m in 2025 and £61.9m in 2024 (2025 p29). Under the service agreement the company pays the parent an amount equal to that expense (2025 p24).
- **Background (UK law, not from the accounts):** under Part 12 of the Corporation Tax Act 2009, the corporation tax deduction for shares acquired by employees is generally based on the shares' market value when the employee acquires them, not on the accounting charge, so the deduction is larger when the share price has risen since the grant.
- **Other reconciling items** are smaller: adjustments for prior years (£130,528 in 2025, £685,558 in 2024), withholding tax (£341,180 in 2025), foreign tax and expenses not deductible (2025 p30, p31).
- **Pillar Two.** The 2025 accounts say the group calculated a GloBE effective tax rate for each country and that the rate in every country other than Qatar and the UAE was at least 15% (2025 p32). That is a different measure from the accounting rate above. The 2024 charge includes UK Multinational Top-up Tax of £23,147 (2025 p30).

## Earlier years

- **2014 to 2019:** in the profit years the charge was above the standard rate, and in 2018 there was a charge despite a loss. Expenses not deductible for tax added to the charge each year, notably the impairments at the subsidiary Palantir Technologies UK - Eagle, Ltd. (£18.6m in 2018 and £18.1m in 2019), which the accounts say is not under a cost-plus agreement (2018 p4, p22; 2019 p4, p28). Over these years the accounts carried a growing deferred tax asset, mainly "employee share acquisition relief": £0.98m at the end of 2014 and £17.6m at the end of 2019 (2014 p17; 2019 p31).
- **2020:** the deferred tax asset grew to £32.0m (£26.1m employee share acquisition relief, £4.9m net operating loss), giving a net tax credit of £14.15m (2020 p28, p29).
- **2021:** the whole £32.0m asset was written off, which made the tax charge (£22.97m) larger than the profit (2021 p31, p32). No deferred tax asset has been recognised since.

## Gaps, conflicts and checks

- **Blank cells:** UK turnover for 2010 to 2015 (the accounts give only percentages; for 2015, the 2015 accounts say 96% of turnover came from outside the UK while the 2016 accounts show 2015 UK turnover as nil); tax at the standard rate for 2010 to 2013 (the small-company accounts have no reconciliation); the standard rate for 2010 and 2011; average employees for 2010 to 2012 (not disclosed).
- **2017:** the filing is inconsistent. The income statement shows a tax charge of £3,931,334, but the tax note on p22 shows £4,010,991, with UK corporation tax of £6,564,121 (checked against the page image). The 2018 accounts (p24) restate the 2017 note to agree with the income statement, and the CSV uses those restated figures.
- **2017 and 2018:** the reconciliations in the 2018 accounts start from a profit of £6.0m for 2017 and a loss of £12.8m for 2018, not the income-statement figures (£10.0m and a loss of £12.6m); the 2017 accounts' own reconciliation starts from £10.4m (2017 p22; 2018 p24).
- **2021:** the 2021 accounts describe the 72% of turnover from outside the UK as services to the parent; the 2022 accounts give the 2021 share of services to the parent as 37%.
- **2013:** the CSV uses the figures as first filed; the restated figures are in the row's notes.
- Every income statement, turnover split, employee total and tax note was re-added to check the OCR; all agree except as noted above. Where OCR misread a figure that is used, it was checked against the page image and a note is added at the end of that page's OCR file (2025 p29, 2020 p27, 2015 p11 and p20, 2014 p16, 2012 p11).
